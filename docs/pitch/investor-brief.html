Executive summary

India's local businesses need a simple way online. Not another marketplace.

More than 12 million kirana stores still hold about 91% of India's grocery retail, but quick-commerce apps are taking share in cities, and most neighbourhood businesses have no online storefront, no booking system and no way to be found nearby. Vocal For Local gives them one link where customers browse, order or book. The order arrives on the owner's WhatsApp, with no commission and no app to install.

Problem

  • Local shops lose customers to apps that charge commissions and run dark stores. Quick commerce reportedly took 25–30% of kirana sales in cities in 2024.
  • About 70% of MSMEs still rely on conventional customer engagement; only about 13% use social or digital marketing (SIDBI, May 2025).

Answers to the three questions investors always ask

"Why won't WhatsApp Business do this?"

WhatsApp Business offers a catalog, but no cart with totals, delivery fees or minimum orders, no order dashboard, no appointment slots, no 5 km discovery, no Excel import and no store website or SEO. We sit on top of WhatsApp and add the commerce layer.

"Why will a kirana pay?"

The free tier proves value (orders) first. Owners upgrade when they hit 50 products or want automated reminders, their own domain or promotion to nearby shoppers. ₹299 is less than one lost order a day.

"What stops copycats?"

Distribution, not code: dense neighbourhood clusters, where every store's link markets the platform to its customers; vernacular trust; distributor partnerships; and a growing local directory where shoppers start their search.

01 · Problem

01Neighbourhood businesses are offline and under pressure

Kirana & local retail

  • Huge but fragmented: industry estimates put India's kirana stores at 12 million or more, and Redseer found kiranas still held about 91% of grocery retail in 2025.
  • Losing share in cities: AICPDF, the distributors' federation, says about 2 lakh kiranas closed in a year amid aggressive quick-commerce expansion. 45% of closures were in metros, and 25–30% of kirana sales moved to quick commerce in 2024.
  • Digital gap: about 36% of retail MSMEs had no online presence (FIRST survey, 2023). About 70% of MSMEs still use only conventional customer engagement (SIDBI, May 2025).
  • Wrong tools: store builders are built for D2C brands, in English, charging monthly fees plus per-order fees (e.g. Dukaan's 1.99–4.99%). Marketplaces charge high commissions and own the customer relationship.

Salons, spas & small clinics

  • Large, growing segment: India's beauty salon market is estimated at roughly US$8–12.5 billion in 2025, growing at about 8% CAGR, and dominated by independent neighbourhood salons. Branded chains have only hundreds of outlets each.
  • Bookings by phone and walk-in: no live availability, double bookings, idle chairs and no-shows. Staff spend time on calls, not customers.
  • Small clinics (dentists, physiotherapists) face the same scheduling problem. Large doctor-booking platforms focus on doctors' discovery, not a small clinic's own booking page.

The customer's side

Customers want to order from the shop they trust, or book their regular stylist, from their phone, in their language, and see what's genuinely near them rather than a city-wide list.

The gap: a free, vernacular, WhatsApp-native way for any local business to sell or take bookings online, without giving up margin or customers.
02 · Solution

02One link. Orders and bookings on WhatsApp.

🛒 Store mode (kirana, bakery, florist, electricals, pharmacy…)

  1. Register with a 10-digit mobile and OTP, then choose "Store".
  2. Add products: manually, from Excel/CSV, or by pasting a WhatsApp price list. Only name, MRP and price are required.
  3. Set your shop location on Google Maps. A quick platform review keeps listings genuine.
  4. Share the link. Customers browse, add to bag and tap Place order via WhatsApp. You get an itemised bill (items, savings, delivery fee, address) on WhatsApp. Pay on delivery: cash or UPI.

📅 Appointment mode (salon, spa, beauty parlour, dentist, physio)

  1. Register and choose "Appointments".
  2. Add services (Haircut 30 min ₹250, Hair Spa 60 min ₹900…) and specialists with weekly hours.
  3. Customers book: service → specialist (or "any") → date → a free slot. The slot is held instantly, so two people can never get the same time.
  4. The customer sends the booking on WhatsApp. The owner confirms in one tap and replies with a pre-filled message in the customer's language.

Built for Bharat

🗣️ 23 languages

English plus all 22 scheduled languages, including right-to-left Urdu, Kashmiri and Sindhi, and Santali in Ol Chiki. Order messages follow the customer's language.

📶 Works on weak networks

Installable PWA. The catalog is cached on the phone for 30 minutes, so customers can keep ordering offline and orders sync when the network returns.

📍 Hyperlocal by design

Shoppers see only businesses within 5 km, sorted by distance. Merchants pin their exact location with Google Maps or GPS.

💸 Zero commission

Customers pay the shop directly by UPI or cash. We never touch the money or take a cut.

🔐 Trust & safety

Mobile OTP login, mandatory store approval, verified WhatsApp numbers, and abuse limits on bookings and OTPs.

🔎 Found on Google

Every store page is search-optimised, with structured data such as LocalBusiness, Product and Service, and WhatsApp-friendly link previews.

04 · Innovation & technology

04What's new here (for DPIIT recognition)

Vocal For Local is a new product and process for local commerce. It combines a messaging-native checkout, vernacular-first design and conflict-free scheduling in a way that works for micro-businesses with no technical skills and low-cost phones.

07 · Go-to-market

07Win one neighbourhood at a time

Because shoppers only see businesses within 5 km, we don't need to be everywhere: we need to be dense. Each cluster is a 5 km circle with 150–300 onboarded businesses, enough for shoppers to make it their first stop.

Acquisition channels

  1. Field champions: local youth (commission-based) onboard shops in person in 10 minutes. They paste the WhatsApp price list, take photos and share the link with the owner's customers.
  2. Built-in virality: every order and booking message carries the store link and "Powered by Vocal For Local". Customers become merchants' word of mouth.
  3. Distributor & FMCG partners: distributors already visit thousands of kiranas weekly. Partner to onboard their retailer network (B2B2C).
  4. Associations & government programmes: traders' associations, salon guilds, Udyam/MSME facilitation centres, ONDC seller enablement.
  5. Search & content: vernacular SEO ("free online store for kirana", "book salon near me") and WhatsApp status creatives.
